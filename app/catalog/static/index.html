Find data across different organisations and see what you may do with it

Partners each publish a DCAT-AP catalogue with ODRL access terms. This catalogue harvests them and checks them against the agreed minimum.

All partner data is mock.

How this works
What exists

Catalogue · DCAT-AP

Each partner publishes one file, catalog.jsonld: a dcat:Catalog listing its datasets (and software as dcat:Resource). DCAT describes assets at dataset level — who, what, when, where, licence, where to get it — not what is inside them.

What you may do

Access terms · ODRL

Every asset carries a machine-readable offer: permissions, prohibitions, constraints (purpose, place of use) and duties. The catalogue evaluates it against your request. It states the terms; enforcing them is a connector's job.

What's inside

Detailed views · CDIF, CodeMeta

Generated from the same record, not published separately: CDIF for data (variables, units) and CodeMeta for software. Richer domain metadata (e.g. RO-Crate packages, Darwin Core) stays with the partner and is linked, not copied.

What we agree on

Minimum · SHACL + vocabularies

A small shape every catalogue must meet, and shared terms: keywords and purposes link to concepts found via ontology lookup services (AgroPortal, BioPortal/EDAM, NERC Vocabulary Server, AGROVOC) — later via the DigiLab Vocabulary Hub. The linked terms are examples only.

Federation: metadata, access terms and (later) proofs of identity travel between partners; the data stays where it is. Each partner keeps its own systems and richer metadata — DigiLab only agrees the envelope. Standards & mappings →
